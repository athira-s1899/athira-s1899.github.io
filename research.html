Research Overview

Neutron stars offer a unique environment for exploring the properties of matter at densities far beyond those accessible in terrestrial experiments. Understanding their internal composition and equation of state (EOS) provides important insights into the behaviour of strongly interacting matter under extreme conditions.

Theoretical investigations based on relativistic mean-field (RMF) models provide a framework for studying dense matter containing nucleons, hyperons and antikaons. Particular attention is given to meson-baryon interactions and flavour symmetry prescriptions, including SU(6) and SU(3), and their influence on neutron-star composition, structure and observable properties.

Additional research directions include neutron-star oscillations and rotational stability, with particular emphasis on r-mode instabilities, bulk viscosity and their implications for gravitational-wave astrophysics.

01. Neutron Stars and Dense Matter

Neutron stars provide a natural laboratory for investigating matter at supranuclear densities. The equation of state of dense matter plays a crucial role in determining their mass, radius, internal composition and maximum mass. Relativistic mean-field models offer a theoretical framework for exploring these properties and understanding the behaviour of matter under extreme conditions.

Internal structure of a neutron star

02. Hyperonic Matter and SU(3) Flavour Symmetry

At high densities, the appearance of hyperons introduces additional degrees of freedom into neutron-star matter. Their interactions with scalar and vector mesons significantly influence the equation of state and the resulting stellar properties. Different flavour symmetry prescriptions, particularly SU(6) and SU(3), provide alternative descriptions of the hyperonic interaction sector and its implications for dense matter.

Hyperons and strange baryonic matter

03. Antikaon Condensation

The possible emergence of antikaon condensates at high densities represents an interesting aspect of exotic neutron-star matter. The strength of kaon-meson interactions and the underlying SU(3) flavour symmetry play important roles in determining the onset of condensation. Such phases can modify the particle composition, equation of state and macroscopic properties of neutron stars.

Possible antikaon-condensed phase

04. Neutron-Star Oscillations and r-mode Instability

Oscillation modes of rotating neutron stars provide a valuable probe of their internal composition and dissipative properties. In particular, r-mode instabilities arise through the interplay between gravitational radiation and viscous dissipation. Bulk viscosity associated with weak interactions involving hyperons plays an important role in determining the stability of these modes and the corresponding instability windows.

Velocity field associated with r-mode oscillations

05. Gravitational Waves and Multimessenger Astrophysics

Gravitational-wave observations of compact stellar systems provide an important avenue for probing the properties of neutron-star matter. The equation of state and internal composition influence stellar structure, tidal properties and the dynamics of compact binaries. Connecting theoretical models of dense matter with gravitational-wave observations offers opportunities to constrain the physics of matter under extreme conditions.
